# Random Arena fighter ideas

This is the theme-first fighter bench. A fighter should feel like a character before it feels like a rules package.

## Design order

1. Pick a recognizable archetype, personality, or dramatic scene.
2. Define its silhouette, movement, sound, and emotional rhythm.
3. Choose one central combat verb that expresses that character.
4. Add no more than three named moments or outcomes around that verb.
5. Make the complete loop explainable in one short paragraph and roughly three to five info blocks.

Spider is the target complexity ceiling: Grapple can Swing, Perch, or Web Pull, and every outcome belongs to the same fantasy. Avoid kits like Ronin where several defenses, vulnerabilities, counters, stacks, and exceptions compete for attention.

## Implemented from this bench

### Stillpoint — the meditating master

A serene martial-arts archetype. Stillpoint stops to Meditate, risks being Disturbed, and earns one explosive Palm Strike by completing the ritual. The animation should sell absolute calm followed by a single impossible burst of force.

### Ascendant — the fallen hero

A mythic guardian with one dramatic Ascension. Its heavy stone body dies once, shatters, and reveals a small luminous glass cannon in an Ascended form. The entire character is the contrast between its patient first life and desperate second life.

### Luchador — the arena showman

A masked wrestling hero who treats every fight as the main event. A clean collision becomes a Clinch, a visible spin, and a Ring Toss that sends both fighters in opposite directions. The spectacle is the mechanic.

## High-priority concepts

### Neon — the arena idol

A glamorous stage performer who plants itself, charges under a spotlight, and sweeps one bright laser across the arena. The opponent can dodge the telegraphed beam; Neon wins by turning the ring into its stage.

### Shogun — the cyber ninja

A neon assassin throws one data blade into arena geometry, then teleports to it and performs a perpendicular slash. The embedded blade clearly forecasts where the attack will originate.

### Capo — the crime boss

A physically unimpressive boss protected by one loyal enforcer. The enforcer intercepts a major hit, retaliates against the attacker, and then leaves the boss exposed while it recovers.

### Crystal — the unstable prodigy

Taking damage grows a dangerous crystal shell around the fighter. At maximum growth the shell bursts into radial shards, leaving Crystal tiny and vulnerable until the cycle begins again.

### Marshal — the uncompromising lawman

Marshal periodically draws a bright boundary across the arena and declares it closed. Crossing that line during the warning window triggers an immediate intercepting charge.

### Maestro — the theatrical performer

Every successful collision advances a visible three-beat song: Tap, Crescendo, Bass Drop. Bass Drop releases a circular knockback wave and resets the rhythm.

### Undertaker — the unstoppable slasher

A slow horror archetype that periodically disappears during a brief arena flicker and reappears behind the opponent. Its next collision becomes Ambush; missing the approach wastes it.

### Groundskeeper — the furious survivalist

Plants one prized root that slowly becomes a healing patch. If the opponent destroys it, Groundskeeper enters a short territorial rage and charges them down.

### Reactor — the barely contained monster

Incoming damage fills a visible radiation meter. At capacity, Reactor enters Meltdown: it glows, accelerates, and leaves a damaging trail until the stored energy burns out.

## Additional character-first concepts

### Fixer — the meticulous cleaner

A quiet underworld professional who marks one opponent or hazard as a Contract. Touching the Contract erases the mark in a sharp burst and pays out one precise damage bonus; only one Contract exists at a time.

### Flashbulb — the relentless photographer

Tracks the opponent with a visible camera cone. Holding them in frame finishes a Focus Lock and fires a flash that briefly blinds and reverses their direction.

### Stuntman — the death-defying daredevil

Near misses with walls and hazards build Applause. The next real collision cashes out that applause as bonus damage, rewarding dangerous lines without adding another attack system.

### Quarterback — the play caller

Throws one bouncing ball ahead and immediately rushes after it. Catching its own pass arms a Tackle; the opponent can intercept the pass to deny the play.

### Chef — the furious perfectionist

Tosses a single spinning cleaver that sticks into a wall. Chef chases it down, retrieves it, and gains one empowered Chop, creating a simple throw–retrieve–strike loop.

### Astronaut — the stranded explorer

Fires a tether into a wall and slowly reels toward it while leaving a floating oxygen canister behind. Reconnecting with the canister heals; the opponent can knock it away first.

### Gumshoe — the noir detective

The first collision leaves a Chalk Mark at the scene. If the opponent crosses that mark again, Gumshoe solves the Case and fires a short, unavoidable retaliation.

### Oracle — the impossible predictor

Places a translucent vision of where the opponent will be after a short delay. If the prediction is correct, Fate strikes that location; a miss simply dissolves the vision.

### Marionette — the haunted puppet

Four visible strings connect it to the arena edges. Each wall collision snaps one string and changes its movement angle; losing the final string triggers a brief, wild Unbound form.

### Doppel — the suspicious impostor

Copies the opponent's body color and one clearly displayed core stat at the start of the fight. Halfway through, it discards the disguise and steals a different stat. It never copies full abilities.

### Headliner — the washed-up action hero

Begins each fight with one enormous explosive entrance that may hit or miss. Afterward it becomes an ordinary underdog, trying to survive long enough for a smaller comeback encore.

### Beekeeper — the eccentric swarm keeper

Carries a visible hive that releases bees whenever Beekeeper is struck. The swarm circles the attacker briefly and nudges it off course before returning home.

### Duelist — the fencer

Extends a very thin thrust only while moving toward the opponent. A tip hit deals major damage; body contact is weak. Unlike Saber, the weapon does not rotate—the character is about spacing and approach direction.

### Moonwalker — the stylish escape artist

Always slides backward relative to the opponent while charging a visible beat. On the beat, it reverses direction for one fast Shoulder Check, then resumes retreating.

## Parking-lot rule

If a concept needs more than one paragraph to explain, reduce it before implementation. Extra personality should come from pose, timing, effects, sounds, and strong interactions—not additional exceptions.
